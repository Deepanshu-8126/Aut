# FUNCYOUS — Deep 3D Visual Explainer Playbook

**Scope:** Reusable production system for explaining body, objects, machines, natural phenomena and other suitable topics. The subject determines the visuals; this is not a fixed mouth/finger template. Communication in English for Funcyous; production notes may be Hinglish. This playbook builds on the inspected Zack D. Films reel as a reference for visual storytelling, not a claim to know its private workflow.

**North star:** Viewer ko sirf fact sunana nahi—action, hidden mechanism, cause-and-effect aur result *dekhkar* samajh aana chahiye. The same recognizable object or character should guide viewers from outside to inside and back whenever that journey fits the topic.

## 2. Mandatory first step: find the explainable mechanism

Before images, animation or sound, write a one-page **Mechanism Card**:
1. **Topic and audience question:** What exactly are we explaining? What everyday observation makes it understandable?
2. **Verified answer in one sentence:** What is the strongest supported cause? What remains uncertain, disputed, variable, model-dependent or unknown?
3. **Object + location:** Which visible body part, object or system contains the mechanism? Where does it begin and end? List spatial orientation so the viewer never gets lost.
4. **Observable input:** What physically happens first? Who or what acts? Identify one real, visible action rather than a generic title.
5. **Hidden chain:** Input → mechanism A → mechanism B → result. Identify moving components, changes in material/state/pressure/flow and time. State if causal links are only hypotheses.
6. **Visible outcome/payoff:** What can the audience actually see? Avoid substituting a claimed result for demonstrated evidence.
7. **Potential misconception / safety:** What oversimplification would mislead? Are there medical, dangerous activity, engineering or historical caveats? Do not recommend an unsafe experiment.
8. **Sources and rights:** Record two credible primary/authoritative sources where practical, date retrieved, claims they support, plus rights of any footage/music/SFX. Separate observation, inference and hypothesis.
9. **Visual translation:** For each cause/effect, what changes on screen? If the proposed shot has only an arrow, label or zoom, add the real visual event it explains.

Do not generate a polished but scientifically wrong picture first and invent the story around it. If evidence is ambiguous, say so in the narration and keep the visual explicitly illustrative.

## 3. Core visual storytelling grammar

**A. Observable action:** Establish a character or object actually doing something meaningful. Hands, facial reactions, mechanism parts, moving water or changing material must have a reason to move.

**B. Follow the question:** Move camera framing toward the precise location being discussed. On every shot ask: “Why is the camera here now?” The camera should direct attention or reveal new information—not simply keep the frame moving.

**C. Enter / reveal:** Where helpful, transition from the outside to an internal view of the *same* object. Preserve orientation, colours, silhouette and scale cues. Use exploded/cutaway/macroscopic or microscopic views only if they clarify the topic. Some subjects are best explained through a process, environment, map or comparison rather than an internal cutaway.

**D. Animate the mechanism:** Show actual parts changing relative to each other—constriction, pumping, mixing, transfer, bending, evaporation, chemical or structural changes—only when the science supports the depiction. Labels and particles help point out the event; they are not a substitute for it.

**E. Show consequence:** Make the next change happen because of the prior change. Keep the important transformation visible long enough to register. Use a visual comparison or split states only when necessary; don't default to separate flat diagrams.

**F. Return / payoff:** Return to the context or show the observable result. Maintain continuity and explain whether a proposed function is certain or uncertain.

**Rule:** One picture + fake zoom + blinking ≠ alive; a moving arrow over a static picture ≠ animated anatomy; a 2D insert ≠ true 3D geometry. Be explicit about which technique was used.

## 4. Design system — recognizable, original and topic-specific

### Character and environment
- Keep the selected original Funcyous character consistent across shots when a character helps. Match face, hair, body proportions, costume, lighting, object position and background. No famous characters or reference-creator likeness/branding.
- Generate or model the necessary **intentional poses**, not many near-identical portraits: action start, reaction, inspect, outcome and relevant close-up. Build a shot continuity sheet before animating.
- A scene’s context matters: bathroom for the hand example, a kitchen and dishwasher for a dishwasher, a believable relevant setting for other topics. A clean colored environment can work if it suits the subject; do not default to any one background.
- Hands and contact demand inspection: correct digit count, coherent joints, object weight, grip point, continuous water/object shape. Avoid stretched fingers, liquid stems, face changes and object teleportation.

### Camera / composition
- Choose framing purposefully: establishing context → medium action → close-up → macro/cutaway → return/result. This is a toolkit, not a compulsory sequence or timestamps.
- Keep motion motivated: pan to follow the subject; push in to inspect; orbit only to reveal depth or orientation; pull back to restore context. Keep horizon and spatial direction understandable.
- A literal camera/lens value cannot be copied from a flat reference without evidence. Set approximate values by testing readability, not claiming reverse-engineered settings.
- Use depth, occlusion, consistent perspective, shadows and lighting across transitions. An image-morph or LTX-generated transition is only acceptable if identity and anatomy hold through *all* frames.

### Materials / effects / clarity
- Use credible surface materials and subject-appropriate light: skin, teeth, tissue, metal, glass, water, fabric, particles and gases should not all have the same plastic appearance.
- Maintain clear subject separation and visual hierarchy. Effects may distinguish signals, temperature, flow or force, but specify when colors, arrows, particles and scale are symbolic.
- The graphic must stay anchored to the real object/system. Labels should be short, legible on a phone and should not cover the critical movement.
- The audience should understand the before/after state without relying solely on narration or text. Check by watching the visuals muted, then listening with the screen hidden, then checking full combination.

## 5. Repeatable topic-to-video workflow

### Phase 1 — Research and premise
- Read current Funcyous channel, sound and test rules; check sources and write the Mechanism Card. Confirm topic accuracy, safety and uncertain claims.
- Find a natural hook: everyday observation, unexpected result or specific question. Search suggestions are not proof of search volume, demand or virality.
- Lock English narration, sequence and claim boundaries before animation; thumbnail-first per channel rules. Preserve chosen thumbnail/character and voice unless changed by user.

### Phase 2 — Script and sound map
- Write short linked cause-and-effect clauses: what happens → where → why → visible consequence. Explain unfamiliar words through visible action; avoid unsupported absolutes.
- Set measured narration timing from the selected voice. Create SOUND MAP **before** audio production: narration, approved Funcyous bed, and visual-specific SFX with timing, source, licensing and intended level.
- Do not layer another complete bed over an existing mix. Caption timings require human correction after machine alignment. Original reel audio characteristics are not a license or a required loudness target.

### Phase 3 — Beat board and camera map
For each narration beat create one row containing: exact spoken phrase/timing; claim/source; frame action; exterior/interior location; shot size and camera purpose; motion start/end; graphic/labels; transition and SFX; risk/verification.
- Identify required core shots before asset generation: reference-character action; close-up; inside mechanism; result. Add or remove shots according to the topic, not to fill a fixed quota.
- Track object identity, screen direction, scale, lighting and camera continuity across cuts. Render a low-cost storyboard/motion test before an expensive final.
- Select genuine action points to animate; the scene must not depend on repetitive zoom, blink, static anatomical posters or superficial particle effects.

### Phase 4 — Asset and animation construction
- Select an appropriate method per beat, with explicit honesty:
  1. **True 3D:** geometry, rigs/deformers, materials, real camera paths and lighting. Use when available and warranted; scientific model/mesh must be validated.
  2. **Layered 2.5D / coded animation:** separated surfaces and elements, perspective/occlusion, masks and animated parameters. Can be purposeful, but must not be called a true 3D anatomy rig.
  3. **Image-to-video:** best for character actions and organic movement; scrutinize anatomical consistency and object interaction, especially hands.
  4. **Motion graphics:** arrows, captions and measurement overlays as support for the main visual, never mistaken for real-life measurements.
- Keep a provenance sheet for each asset: origin, tool/model version, prompt, seed when available, license/permissions, date, limitations and which shots use it.
- Kaggle LTX setup has previously run successfully, but model startup, downloads, queue and GPU quota vary per fresh session. Do not assume persisted warm weights or claim a run completed before verifying output.

### Phase 5 — Edit, sound and captions
- Cut to action and speech, not arbitrary time. Maintain causality and re-establish spatial orientation when crossing from exterior to interior. Favour the simplest visual that actually explains the beat.
- Add captions in short readable chunks; preserve speech and safe zones. Ensure hard edits do not truncate speech or sound cues. Keep original voice unchanged unless the user approves.
- Mix only the mapped audio. Document integrated loudness and true peak as technical measurements, not as a substitute for ear review. Verify music/SFX license terms item by item.

### Phase 6 — QC and delivery
**Automated technical checks:** full decode; output dimensions/fps/duration; sample all frames for black/frozen frames; inspect scene boundaries; measure loudness/true peak; check caption coverage, filenames and output hashes. Heuristics identify problems; they do not certify quality or virality.

**Human visual QC:** watch every shot at normal speed and slow speed. Check frame-by-frame around cuts, fast action, hand/object contact and anatomy changes. Is every mechanism visually clear, consistent and scientifically defensible? Is there a meaningful camera move instead of a fake zoom? Does the first second show a relevant action?

**Human audio QC:** full uninterrupted listen on headphones and phone: voice pronunciation, rhythm, intelligibility, music masking, SFX timing/levels, clicks, double beds, audio transitions and ending. Compare caption text and timing to actual words.

**Editorial/rights QC:** source-check every claim; distinguish hypothesis from demonstrated result; inspect anatomy and any real-world context; confirm all licenses and attribution as required. User’s watch/listen and approval matter more than a machine score.

**Delivery:** honest label REVIEW until the human watch/listen, facts and rights checks are complete. Preserve masters, source project, output, script, captions, thumbnail, sources and sound map. If user requests Kaggle, provide the verified private notebook Output link and filename; optionally back up the full kit as a private dataset. Never upload to YouTube without explicit authorization.

## 6. General examples: translate the same method, not the same visuals

### A. Human body: wet-finger wrinkles
- Exterior wet fingertip → consistent skin cutaway → illustrate nerve-related vessel narrowing and reduced volume → surface folds → return to hand. Treat wet-grip function as possible, not proven evolutionary purpose. The cutaway remains simplified unless medically validated.
- The existing coded fingertip graphic explains direction, but it is still a stationary AI anatomical image plus 2D-coded overlays, not a deforming 3D finger. Do not present it as completed true-3D anatomy.

### B. Machine: dishwasher circulation
- Dirty dish at rack → track water from sump to pump → follow path through spray arms and jets → droplets interact with dishes → filtered water returns. Mechanical parts rotate/move in the correct locations. Do not show the tub filling like a bathtub unless the specific model actually does.
- Same camera logic, different environment, materials, part geometry, causes and sound. A pump diagram alone is not a full simulation.

### C. Natural phenomenon: condensation
- Show warm moist air entering an appropriate cooler environment → illustrate cooling and water vapor condensing → visible droplets/fog → return to observation. Use symbols to signal invisible molecules, not misleadingly oversized particles as literal photography.
- Scientific conditions and safe demonstrations must be verified; do not copy the original reel’s medical or physical claims uncritically.

### D. History or unknown mechanism
- Exterior-to-interior is not mandatory. Orient viewer in place/time → show physical object and evidence → separate verified facts from competing hypotheses → end on what remains unknown. Avoid invented reconstructions presented as genuine footage or proof.

## 7. Final approval checklist

- [ ] Topic and one-sentence explanation fact-checked; uncertainty stated.
- [ ] Shot sequence is topic-specific and follows the mechanism.
- [ ] Character/object identity and spatial geography remain consistent.
- [ ] Camera movement has a reason; action and material change are visible.
- [ ] Motion graphics reinforce rather than replace substantive scene animation.
- [ ] If cutaway is schematic or AI-conceptual, label/document it accurately.
- [ ] No copied creator branding, likeness, script, voice or unlicensed assets.
- [ ] SOUND MAP completed first; approved voice/music; SFX and rights verified.
- [ ] Captions checked against listening; fact/caption/audio/visual human QC complete.
- [ ] Full decode, boundaries, frame scan, audio levels and backup/hash checked.
- [ ] Delivered as review unless publication QC and user approval are complete.
