### COLD OPEN (0:00-0:50) — rig: ARCHIVAL (Telstar PD) → black
[M] By 1977, the sky was already crowded. Satellites. Radio. Television. Thousands of signals crossing space every single second... and every one of them had a return address.
[M] Every one... except one.
[F] August 15, 1977. 23:16 Eastern. Delaware, Ohio. Duration: seventy-two seconds. Source: unknown.
[M] This is the story of the strongest candidate for an alien signal ever recorded... and the fifty-year silence that followed. (BEAT) It was found three days late. On a kitchen table.

### CH 1 — THE QUIET CHANNEL (0:50-2:05) — rig: code diagram + mention-lock
[M] To understand what happened, you need to know about one radio frequency. (BEAT) 1420 megahertz. The hydrogen line.
[M] Hydrogen is the most common thing in the universe. And it hums. Every atom of it, everywhere, rings at this exact frequency.
[M] In 1959, two physicists made a famous bet: if an alien civilization ever wanted to be found, this is the channel they would choose. Every astronomer in the galaxy... ours or anyone else's... would know to listen here.
[M] So that's exactly what a giant telescope in Ohio was doing. Night after night. For years. Listening to the quietest channel in the universe... and hearing nothing but static.
[F] Protected frequency. By international law, no human transmitter is allowed to broadcast there.
[M] Remember that. It matters later.

### CH 2 — THE MACHINE (2:05-3:25) — rig: Big Ear photoreal still ALIVE + real photos
[M] They called it Big Ear. Three football fields of aluminum, flat on the ground, staring at the sky over Delaware, Ohio.
[M] It couldn't move. It just let the Earth do the turning... so every object in the sky drifted through its beam for exactly seventy-two seconds. (BEAT) Rise... peak... fade. Like a lighthouse sweeping in reverse.
[M] And here's the detail this whole mystery turns on: Big Ear had two feed horns. Two ears. A few minutes apart. Anything real in the sky should ring BOTH of them. First one... then the other.
[F] Survey mode: unattended. Observers: none. Output: one line printer. Fifty characters per line.
[M] No speakers. No alarms. Nobody listening live. Just a computer... quietly typing numbers onto paper. All night. Every night.

### CH 3 — THE NIGHT (3:25-4:40) — rig: Big Ear night scene (tested) + bell curve
[M] August 15th, 1977. 11:16 p.m.
[M] The dorms nearby were quiet. The parking lot was empty. Big Ear was pointed at nothing special... a patch of sky in the constellation Sagittarius. Toward the heart of the galaxy.
[M] At 11:16... channel two came alive.
[F] Intensity: six. Then fourteen. Then twenty-six. Peak: thirty times background. A near-perfect bell curve. Duration: seventy-two seconds.
[M] Thirty times louder than the background of the universe. Rising and falling EXACTLY the way a real deep-space source should. Not a glitch. Not a spike. A visitor... passing through the beam.
[M] And no one on Earth heard it. (BEAT) The computer typed six characters... and moved on to the next line.

### CH 4 — THE KITCHEN TABLE (4:40-5:50) — rig: EVIDENCE-CARD (tested: folder, pen, Wow!)
[M] Three days later. A professor named Jerry Ehman sits down at his kitchen table with a stack of printouts. Volunteer work. Unpaid. Coffee, probably.
[M] Page after page of ones and twos. Static. Noise. Nothing. And then... in column two... his eyes catch something that should not exist.
[F] Six. E. Q. U. J. Five.
[M] Ehman had been doing this for years. He knew what noise looked like. This was not noise. (BEAT) He grabbed a red pen. Circled six characters. And wrote the only word that fit.
[M] ...Wow.
[M] Not a codename. Not a press release. Just one man, alone in a kitchen... realizing he might be the first human in history reading a message from somewhere else.

### CH 5 — WHAT 6EQUJ5 ACTUALLY SAYS (5:50-6:40) — rig: macro insert + decode overlay
[M] Those six characters are not a word. They're a volume meter.
[F] Zero through nine... then A through Z. Each character: one twelve-second sample. U equals intensity thirty.
[M] Six-E-Q-U-J-five is a mountain drawn in text. Quiet... loud... DEAFENING... loud... quiet. Seventy-two seconds, exactly matching Earth's rotation through the beam.
[M] Whatever this was... it wasn't moving like a plane. It wasn't flickering like interference. It sat still in deep space... while our planet turned past it.

### CH 6 — THE SUSPECTS (6:40-8:50) — rig: CORKBOARD (tested) — stamps per ruled-out
[M] Okay. Before anyone says aliens... you rule out everything else. One by one.
[M] Suspect one: a satellite. (BEAT) Except... 1420 is a protected band. Nothing transmits there. And a satellite crossing the beam would NOT fake a perfect fixed-star bell curve.
[F] Ruled out.
[M] Suspect two: an aircraft. Same problem. Planes move. This didn't.
[F] Ruled out.
[M] Suspect three: a ground signal bouncing off space junk. The reflection would smear. This was clean.
[F] Ruled out.
[M] Suspect four came forty years later: two comets, trailing hydrogen clouds, supposedly in that patch of sky in 1977. Headlines said: solved.
[M] One problem. Astronomers checked. The comets were NOT in the beam that night. And comets don't switch off. The hypothesis died within a year.
[F] Ruled out.
[M] Which leaves the board... with one card. (BEAT) No known source.

### CH 7 — THE TWO PROBLEMS NOBODY CAN SOLVE (8:50-10:20) — rig: diagram + silence beats
[M] Now the part that keeps astronomers up at night. Two problems.
[M] Problem one: the second horn. Remember... anything real should ring both ears, minutes apart. (BEAT) The Wow signal hit ONE horn. One. The other heard... nothing.
[M] Either it switched on and off within minutes... or something stranger. To this day, nobody even knows WHICH of the two horns caught it.
[M] Problem two: it never came back.
[F] Follow-up searches: more than one hundred. Instruments: the Very Large Array. META. Big Ear itself, for decades. Result: silence.
[M] Real astrophysical sources repeat. Pulsars pulse. Quasars stay on. This... rang once... like a bell struck a single time... and went quiet for fifty years.

### CH 8 — WHAT HAPPENED TO THE EAR (10:20-11:30) — rig: archival montage drive
[M] You'd think the telescope that caught it would be a monument.
[M] In 1998... Big Ear was torn down. (BEAT) The land was sold... to expand a golf course.
[M] The most famous radio telescope in SETI history is now... a fairway. The exact spot where the signal arrived has a sand trap on it.
[F] Dr. Jerry Ehman kept the original printout. He donated it to the Ohio History Connection. The red ink has not faded.
[M] And Ehman himself? The man who wrote "Wow!"... became its most careful skeptic. For decades he refused to say aliens. He only ever said: we ruled out everything we could think of. What remains... remains.

### CH 9 — THE QUESTION (11:30-12:40) — rig: night sky + slow pull + thumbnail frame
[M] So here's where it lands. The signal was real. It was strong. It came from the direction of Sagittarius... from a patch of sky with no star, no planet, no satellite, no comet... nothing we know of.
[M] It chose the one channel the universe agreed to keep quiet. It rose and fell like a perfect visitor. It rang one ear and not the other. And in fifty years... it has never spoken again.
[M] Maybe it was a phenomenon we haven't discovered yet. That's happened before. (BEAT) Or maybe... for seventy-two seconds in 1977... someone pointed at a small blue planet... said one word... and hung up.
[M] If that's true, the question isn't what they said. (BEAT) The question is whether anyone was supposed to be listening... and whether they'll ever... call back.
[M] (final, near-whisper) The line is still open. 1420 megahertz. The quietest channel in the universe.
