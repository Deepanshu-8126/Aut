## 2. One-line discovery

The reel does not explain an invisible mechanism with an unrelated icon on a dark background. It keeps an identifiable character and a recognizable **same mouth**, moves from the outside toward that mouth, reveals its interior, and makes particles, pressure and airflow change *inside the same space*, timed to narration. Then it returns outside for the result.

**Structure:** action → close-up → reveal inside → show cause → show resulting mechanism → exit body → payoff. The viewer sees where and why something happens, not merely a labeled diagram.

## 3. Visual grammar

### Character and world
- One stylized yet textured 3D-looking character: consistent face, haircut, red shirt and body proportions. Cyan backdrop isolates the action; red shirt provides strong colour separation. This is a rendered-looking illustration, not evidence of the original creator’s actual production pipeline.
- Facial movement is story: breathing, cheeks filling, lips closing, eyes narrowing during pressure and expression changing at the end. Hands support the initial breath. The character is not a motionless portrait with only the image enlarged.
- Identity stays legible when the shot changes from front portrait to side mouth and back. Reusing the shirt/logo or exact character would not be appropriate for Funcyous; create an original character instead.

### Anatomy and spatial continuity
- Around 3–4 s, the mouth area becomes a circular/oval cutaway in the side of the head, rather than switching to an unrelated flat chart. Teeth, tongue, jaw and adjacent structures provide location cues.
- At 10–13 s the view moves closer inside the mouth; droplets are shown relative to tongue and teeth. At about 13–16 s the wider cutaway returns, with a humidity gauge and warm glow attached to the same anatomical setting.
- Cutaway geometry and scientific visuals are illustrative; the video alone does not prove anatomical or scientific accuracy. Fact-check before adopting any explanation.

### Camera: observed screen changes, not claimed production settings
- **Scale shifts by narrative purpose.** Opening: upper-body character for the action; by ~1.5–2.5 s: face close-up to read lips and cheeks; ~3–4 s: side profile and internal mouth; ~10–12.5 s: macro interior; ~16–20 s: return to face; ~20–23 s: back inside; ~24–30 s: release and outside airflow; ~31–32.5 s: macro particle view; ~33–36.5 s: character payoff.
- **Direction has meaning:** move toward the object when asking the viewer to inspect it; return outward to show the result. Some transitions appear to blend/rotate between views; do not claim an exact 3D camera move, lens, focal length, or editing software from the final video alone.
- **Framing changes together with information.** A camera move that merely zooms a still without a new visual event is not equivalent. Foreground, internal anatomy, animated overlays and subject action change within the shot.
- Subject normally occupies the center or a side with clear negative space for the action. At ~27–30 s, the character is to the right while the cloud/air-mixing diagram occupies the left.

### Materials, lighting, colour
- Skin and oral surfaces carry varied highlights, shading and texture; teeth and tongue are easy to distinguish. The cavity is darker, allowing bright effects to stand out. These are observations, not a measured shader recipe.
- Cyan background and red clothing give a clean high-contrast base. Warm orange/pink glows mark warm or pressurized air, while cooler blues and white particles help distinguish the surrounding air and droplets. Colours are visual explanations, not measured temperatures.
- Small graphics are integrated into the same world: arrows around lips, pressure arrows within mouth, an overlaid humidity gauge, and rotating airflow arrows. They complement the 3D scene rather than replacing the entire environment.

### Text and captions
- The video uses short white lower-screen captions with dark edging/shadow, typically a few words at a time, following the spoken explanation. Some explanatory graphic labels such as the humidity gauge are part of the scene.
- The opening immediately shows the action and spoken words; it does not rely on a large standalone headline or a long title card. Caption exact font cannot be established reliably from compressed frames.
- Keep text mobile-readable and away from essential action; captions should not obscure the mouth, or, in our project, the fingertips.

## 6. What our fingers edit got wrong, compared with this reel

1. At about 6 s, the prior finger cutaway appeared as a **still image**. A moving overlay did not make the actual finger, tissue or camera genuinely move.
2. At 8–14 s, the earlier edit switched to an isolated, flat vessel diagram; it lost the fingertip’s spatial continuity. The newer coded overlay is clearer, but the vessel inset and line remain schematic and not a true 3D cutaway.
3. The prior editing relied on repeated similar character shots, rather than a planned exterior-to-interior-to-exterior journey. Marble grip is only a conceptual illustration and must not prove evolutionary purpose.
4. The character shots contain real generated motion, but hand consistency remains to be reviewed; the motion was generated at 512 × 896 and upscaled to 1080 × 1920.
5. Labels and overlays should be subordinate to the mechanism: the visual change itself needs to make the cause legible even without reading a title.

## 7. Practical, original fingers production plan

**Do not reproduce the reference’s branding, character, narration, exact image compositions, music, or voice.** Use the existing red-haired Funcyous character and an original bathroom and material palette.

- **0–3 s / outside:** same character lifts wet hand, reacts; actual wrist/finger motion. Move from medium shot toward fingertip as the hand rises. Verify finger count and joints frame by frame.
- **3–6 s / close-up:** dedicated matching hand close-up with visibly wrinkled fingertips. Establish orientation before entering the skin; don't use an unrelated hand.
- **6–9 s / enter:** transition from the surface to a consistent side cutaway of that same finger. Show the nerve signal as a clearly labelled illustration; place vessels in plausible tissue, not as an invented literal scan. Avoid implying electricity is physically travelling down a blood vessel.
- **9–12 s / mechanism:** a vessel visibly contracts in context; surrounding tissue volume changes. Animate a vessel and tissue with consistent lighting, perspective and occlusion. A camera move must reveal something new; zoom alone does not count.
- **12–14 s / result:** relate lower tissue volume to visible surface folds in the same cutaway; use a clear simplified educational visual, not an unsupported exact medical simulation. Take care not to claim the entire skin is literally pulled by a single vessel.
- **14–21 s / possible function:** transition back to the original hand and a real wet-object handling action with believable contact. Voice and text must say evidence suggests possible benefit, not that the evolutionary reason is proven.
- **21–end / payoff:** return to character and hand. Finish on a meaningful reaction or loop, not a recycled movement that contradicts the narration.

### Coding / generation design
- Build a cohesive shot using a single matched character, background, exterior finger, and cutaway. Ideally a controllable layered 2.5D/3D scene with textured meshes or carefully matched layers; if true geometry or physically correct deformation isn't available, label it as a simplified illustrative cutaway.
- Code purposeful motion: reveal mask along the finger surface; tracked signal; vessel width contraction; nearby tissue deformation; evolving surface wrinkles; subtle light/particles; camera travel across layers. Use synchronized keyframes based on narration, not endless loops.
- AI image-to-video can generate hand acting, but outputs must be checked for six fingers, fused joints, floaty grip, warped face and changing background. It is not a substitute for a medically validated anatomical model.
- Render each shot, inspect a dense contact sheet and the whole moving sequence at normal and slow speed; review captions and audio at full length, validate mechanism and check music/effect licensing before upload.
