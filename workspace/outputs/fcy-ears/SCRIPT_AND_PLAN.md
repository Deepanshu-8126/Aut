# Why Do Your Ears Pop on an Airplane? — Funcyous script & production plan

## English narration (draft, 96 words)

Ever felt your ears pop when a plane lands? As the plane descends, the air pressure in the cabin rises. But the air behind your eardrum may not adjust as quickly. The higher pressure outside pushes your eardrum inward, making your ear feel blocked. A narrow passage connects your middle ear to the back of your nose. Normally, this tube stays closed. But when you swallow or yawn, tiny muscles briefly open it, letting air into your middle ear. As the pressure balances, your eardrum can move more freely, and you may hear that little pop.

## Accuracy / visual constraints
- This script describes a pressure mismatch specifically during descent; do not imply the same inward pressure direction during ascent. Cabin pressure is regulated but changes as the aircraft descends.
- The middle ear is the air-filled space behind the eardrum; the Eustachian tube connects it to the back of the nose/throat. Swallowing can briefly open it; air can enter during descent, allowing pressures to equalize. Not everyone feels or hears a pop and pressure may not equalize immediately, especially if the tube is blocked.
