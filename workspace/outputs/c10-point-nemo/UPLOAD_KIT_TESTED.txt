DESCRIPTION
In the summer of 1997, deep-sea hydrophones placed thousands of miles apart across the South Pacific detected an ultra-low frequency sound that baffled oceanographers.

The sound — dubbed "The Bloop" — was louder than any known marine animal and was traced near Point Nemo, the most isolated coordinates on planet Earth (2,688 km from any human land).

While official reports attribute the acoustic profile to massive Antarctic icequakes (cryoseisms), the sheer acoustic range and eerie underwater waveform remain one of modern history's greatest oceanic mysteries.
